01 · THE SETUP

Money arrived faster than meaning.

In four years, annual capital into the AI stack rose more than tenfold. The 2023 inflection was not a wave — it was a regime change. Foundation labs absorbed the headlines; infrastructure quietly absorbed the cash.

What the stream chart makes plain is that the boom was never one market. It was four — model, infrastructure, application, tooling — each with its own physics of cost, defensibility, and decay.

Not all revenue is worth the same multiple — and the market knows it.

Two lenses on the same question. Left: what investors pay per dollar of recurring revenue, by category. Right: the revenue itself, plotted on the only axes that matter — gross margin against net retention. The top-right quadrant is where durable companies live.

06 · POSITIONING

Growth is cheap. Defensibility is the whole game.

Plot every company on two axes — how fast it is growing, and how hard it would be to replace — and the landscape resolves into four fates. The upper-right is rare and richly rewarded. The upper-left, fast but undefended, is where most application startups sit: thrilling charts, borrowed moats.

The model layer enjoys structural defensibility through capital and talent. The application layer must manufacture its own — through data, workflow lock-in, or distribution it does not rent.

07 · DEMAND SIGNALS

Pilots are easy. The question is what survives renewal.

Enterprise adoption by use case, traced across twelve quarters. The curves diverge sharply: code assistance and support automation cleared procurement and stuck; general-purpose agents drew the most noise and the least durable spend.

08 · THE COMPUTE TAX

The cost of intelligence is collapsing. So is the moat it once bought.

The price of a unit of inference has fallen roughly an order of magnitude a year. For users, a gift. For model companies, an erosion — yesterday's frontier becomes today's commodity, and the premium for being first decays on a half-life.

Beneath every application sits a dependency it does not control: the model, the cloud, the accelerator, the power. Margins at the top are hostage to costs at the bottom.

10 · CONSOLIDATION

When the story breaks, the assets move up the stack.

The acquisition map shows where stranded talent and technology are flowing. Hyperscalers and incumbent platforms are the buyers of last resort; the application tail is the seller. The chord on the left traces the flow between layers; the ledger on the right names the pattern.

11 · THE THESIS

Follow the dollar of end-user value. See who keeps it.

Of every dollar a customer pays for an AI-powered outcome, trace what each layer retains after it pays the layer below. The waterfall is the argument of this dossier in a single chart.

Value accrues to defensible demand — not to the layer with the most capital.

The infrastructure layer captures the most today because it owns the scarcest input. The model layer captures less than its valuations imply, because its product commoditizes. And the application layer keeps only what its workflow lock-in can defend.

The durable winners will be the few that own a proprietary loop — distribution, data, or workflow — that does not reprice every nine months. Everyone else is renting their moat from the floor below.
